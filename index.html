    nanoMMO

    The MMO I'd like to see exist, built from scratch in C++, with the engineering and the hard parts out in the open.

    nanoMMO is a devlog where I build an MMO from scratch in C++, with features I'd like to see exist, even if some might be impractical for most games, such as players creating their own skills. It's a learning journey, with the software engineering and the hard parts out in the open, and not always about the gameplay.

    The name is a working title. It takes after nanochat, a repository by Andrej Karpathy, with code that's kept minimal and easy to hack on. That's what I'm going for here as well: a codebase that covers an MMO from one end to the other, which you can read, run yourself, and play with.

    The episodes are on YouTube. The game's code is on GitHub, tagged at each step of each episode, so you can follow along. Its API docs are here, and the reasons it's built the way it is are in its decision records.

    Design choices

    1. 0047A faction is sworn to once, at its herald, guilds have none, and it means nothing in a fight yetLv 97
    2. 0046The servers send their sentences as their English and args, which the client translates
    3. 0045The client translates with gettext's PO files, as it shows each string, and the content stays English
    4. 0044Looks are chosen once, as places in the content's lists, and names show over whoever's pointed at or picked
    5. 0043Craft from the bag, without a station, and a recipe needs all it takes
    6. 0042A war kill counts once, a victim scores once every five minutes, and nobody leaves a war
    7. 0041Guilds at war hurt each other anywhere, for half an hour, and count their kills
    8. 0040Check the content as a graph, with smollerGraph, in the testsLv 90
    9. 0039Objectives with error budgets, and chaos experiments in staging, never in production
