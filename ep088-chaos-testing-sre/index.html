    Chaos testing and site reliability engineering for an indie MMO

    We freeze Redis, MySQL and the login server, and kill the saver, while bots play our indie MMO, and find a sword that existed twice and players dropped while our numbers said all was well, with Google's SRE book as the guide.

    Watch on YouTube

    Chaos testing and site reliability engineering for our indie MMORPG, in staging, with bots. We measure what players get as three service level indicators, ticks on time, logins answered and saves fresh, make them objectives with error budgets in Prometheus, and alert on how fast a budget burns, as Google's SRE Workbook does. Netflix's Chaos Monkey switches off servers in production at random; we run one instance of each world, on one server, so we break things on purpose in staging instead, with a tool that freezes Redis, MySQL, the login server or the telemetry, or kills the saver, one at a time, while bots play. Before any fix, a five-second Redis freeze froze every player and our tick indicator counted one tick missed, twelve seconds dropped everyone, a trade during a one-second Redis outage copied a sword, and a saver killed mid-save left 88 players' rows behind in MySQL. We go through the SRE book chapter by chapter, with what we do of it and what we don't, and the next episode fixes most of what we found. This is episode 88 of a series about building an MMORPG from scratch in C++, with the focus on the engineering around the game.
