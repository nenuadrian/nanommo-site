    Low poly characters for an MMORPG in C++

    The players become low poly people, made of parts the content describes and posed by the bots' bones, each with their own skin and hair, a hull for the outline, and clothes that are items.

    Watch on YouTube

    We replace our MMORPG's Mixamo characters with low poly people, made of parts that a protocol buffer file describes: limbs with round ends and balls, each bound to one bone of the bots' skeletons with raylib's GPU skinning, so their animations still pose them. First a look at what we tried in a lab, people in clothes, one-colour stick figures, a skeleton and a spider with inverse kinematics, and why we kept the people. Each player gets a skin and a way to wear their hair from their id, the outline gets a hull with smooth normals, the server sends what each player wears, and an item's look gets parts of its own. A new player starts in a shirt of their own colour, trousers and shoes, which are items, so the leather cap, tunic, boots and buckler show on whoever wears them too, with a shadow under each figure. This is episode 44 of a series about building an MMORPG from scratch in C++, with the focus on the engineering around the game.
