    MMORPG combat feel in C++ with hit-stop, knockback and a sword combo

    Blows that push monsters back, a moment's hold as each one lands, monsters that flash and rock back, a lit blade and cuts in the air, and a chain of three swings on Space, in our C++ MMORPG.

    Watch on YouTube

    Our MMORPG's fights get some weight. The C++ server lets a skill land more than once, and each blow pushes the monster back over a fifth of a second, eased out, instead of moving it in one tick. The raylib client adds hit-stop on its own clock: each blow that lands holds the player and the monster still for a few frames, longer for a heavier hit, and they catch up after it, so the skill still ends when the server says. The monster lights up through the shader's emission and rocks back away from whoever hit it, the blade lights along its edge, cuts hang in the air with sounds made from code, and Space goes round a chain of three swings. This is episode 61 of a series about building an MMORPG from scratch in C++, with the focus on the engineering around the game.
