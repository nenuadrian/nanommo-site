    MMORPG quest system in C++, with NPCs that give quests

    Quests in the content, given by NPCs that are people now, which ask you to kill a number of monsters, counted on the server, turned in for experience and prizes, and saved.

    Watch on YouTube

    We add quests to our MMORPG: a quest is data, in a protocol buffer file, with the NPC who gives it, how many of which monster it asks you to kill, and the experience and items it gives. The server decides it all: taking a quest from its giver, counting the kills for it, and turning it in for its reward, with the prizes going into a copy of the bag first, so that it's all of them or none. The client gets its quests over TCP, like its bag, with a quest window, a tracker in the corner, and a gold mark over each NPC with a quest for you. The quests are saved as JSON in the player's row in MySQL, through Redis, and the world fuzzer takes and turns them in too. Before that, the NPCs become low poly people, like the players, and we fix what CI found in the last episode's code, with clang-format and clang-tidy. This is episode 45 of a series about building an MMORPG from scratch in C++, with the focus on the engineering around the game.
