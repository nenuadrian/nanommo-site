    Adding factions to an MMORPG

    Three factions in the content, each with a herald who swears players to it for good, checked by the world, saved once through Redis into MySQL, and shown as a diamond in its colour before each sworn player's name.

    Watch on YouTube

    Adding factions to an MMORPG, three sides a player swears to at a herald, once and for good. Factions are content, in a text file with a schema, each with a colour and a herald, an NPC who stands west of where new players start, and the content's checks and its graph make sure each one can be joined. The client sends the herald's id, and the server checks that it's a herald, that the player is within talking reach, and that they haven't sworn already. The faction is saved with the rest of the player through Redis, and the saver writes it into MySQL with COALESCE, only where the row has none, so nothing switches it. Each player's faction goes with their name to everyone near them, and the client draws a diamond in its colour before each sworn name, with a herald's window that asks once more before you swear. The bots swear too, a third to each faction, so a load test's crowd shows all three colours. Factions mean nothing in a fight yet, and guilds have none, so guildmates can be on different sides. This is episode 97 of a series about building an MMORPG from scratch in C++, with the focus on the engineering around the game.
