    Smooth movement for other players in a C++ MMORPG

    Our C++ MMORPG draws the other players and the monsters a tenth of a second in the past, between two worlds it already has, so they walk smoothly on a network that loses packets, and carry on for a moment when worlds go missing.

    Watch on YouTube

    Entity interpolation for our MMORPG, in C++. On a network that loses packets, the other players and the monsters stopped and lurched whenever a world from the server came late or not at all, as the client eased each of them towards the newest place it had. The client now keeps the last second of worlds, works out how far its clock is from the server's from the world that came quickest, and draws everyone else a tenth of a second in the past, between the two worlds on either side of that moment, while your own player stays in the present with last episode's prediction. When the next worlds are lost, the last two carry everyone on the way they were going, for a tenth of a second at most. This is episode 69 of a series about building an MMORPG from scratch in C++, with the focus on the engineering around the game.
