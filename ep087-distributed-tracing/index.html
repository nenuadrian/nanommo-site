    Distributed tracing with OpenTelemetry in a work-in-progress MMO

    Our work-in-progress MMO follows a player's way in, a trade and a deploy through its servers as traces, with a tracer of our own that speaks OpenTelemetry, and the first traces show each MySQL read taking two round trips.

    Watch on YouTube

    Distributed tracing for our MMORPG's servers with OpenTelemetry, Grafana Tempo and Alloy. Metrics say that something got slow, and logs say what one process did, while a trace follows one request through the login server, Redis, MySQL, a world and the saver, and shows where its time went. We write a small tracer of our own in C++ that speaks OpenTelemetry's protocol and W3C Trace Context's traceparent, and carry a player's way in across two servers through the token in Redis, a trade through a Redis stream to the saver, and a deploy to the world's next instance. Then trace ids in the logs, linked both ways in Grafana, and tail sampling in Alloy, which keeps the slow and failed traces. With MySQL two milliseconds away, the first traces showed each statement taking two round trips, so a connection now keeps the reads it's prepared, which halves a player's hello. This is episode 87 of a series about building an MMORPG from scratch in C++, with the focus on the engineering around the game.
